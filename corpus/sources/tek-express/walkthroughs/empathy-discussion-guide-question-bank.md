# Fortive Growth Accelerator empathy interview discussion guide and question bank

## Summary

**Methodology document — not transcript data.** This is the **Fortive Growth Accelerator (GA)** team's standard discussion guide for **contextual + advice empathy interviews**. It codifies the ground rules, interview outline (introduction → contextual walkthrough → wrap-up), key tools (Jobs-to-be-done, Customer Journeyline, Five Why's, Customer Persona template), and a deep question bank organized by category (Background, Responsibilities & Workflow, Social Jobs, Emotional Jobs, Relationships, Trends, IFD pain-point quantification, Persistent Questions, Initial Discovery, Customer Journeyline, Identifying Problems).

The document is the **upstream methodology** for every Subhasis-authored empathy-map deck in the corpus ([`designcon-2023-voc-summary/`](../decks/designcon-2023-voc-summary/_index.md), [`india-customer-voc-2023-march/`](../decks/india-customer-voc-2023-march/_index.md), and [`tek-flow/decks/tekflow-voc-synthesis-2022-12/`](../../tek-flow/decks/tekflow-voc-synthesis-2022-12/_index.md)). It belongs in the same family as the [`Tx-Rx Solutions Platform VOC guide`](../uploads/artifacts/Tx-Rx Solutions Platform VOC guide Draft _08_TEK-CONFIDENTIAL.pdf) (Subhasis's TekExpress-specific VOC methodology document) — both are interview-conduct guides, not VOC findings.

## Goal

Equip Tek (and broader Fortive operating company) UX / product researchers to conduct **contextual + advice interviews** that surface **Jobs-to-be-Done, Customer Personas, and Customer Journeylines** with **functional, social, and emotional job dimensions** — and to **quantify pain via IFD (Intensity · Frequency · Density)** without biasing the customer.

## Starting state

The interviewer has a customer ready for a 1-2 hour interview. The customer has consented to recording. The interviewer has the customer persona template, the JTBD framework, and the journeyline framework in mind. No specific product is named — this is a **cross-product, cross-operating-company** methodology document used by Fortive Growth Accelerator–trained teams.

## Steps

### 1. Ground rules for VOC

The guide opens with three **non-negotiable ground rules** every interviewer must hold:

> *"**No Pitching:** Stay focused on learning, NOT on selling your vision or idea."*
>
> *"**Past & Present:** Keep the conversation in the Past & Present, NOT the future — we don't want customer predictions. No 'Would you…' OR 'Will you…' questions."*

The third "rule" is actually a tool-pack callout (Key Tools & Deliverables) for **JTBD + Customer Persona**, **Customer Journeyline**, **Emotion** (read tone / body language / pitch — *"when to snorkel, when to scuba"*), and **Five Why's** (repeatedly drill to root cause).

### 2. Strong introduction (5-10 minutes)

The introduction sequence:

1. **Introduce self and objectives** — *"set the participant up as a partner in the discovery process."*
2. **Explain the innovation process** without biasing responses.
3. **Promise confidentiality.**
4. **Ask permission to record (audio + video).**
5. **Ask the participant for demographics + introduction.**

The guide provides a **verbatim script** for the introduction:

> *"Hi <insert name>, It's great to meet you and thank you so much for your time today! My name is <insert name> and I'll be interviewing you today together with <insert partner's name>. We're a part of an innovation team looking to learn more about your role, what you do on a day-to-day basis, and your greatest pain points in hopes that we can help solve some of them for you. Right off the bat we wanted to make sure you okay with us recording the interview, I believe this was already mentioned to you, but everything we use here will be confidential and for internal purposes only. We just know you'll be sharing a lot of great insights and we want to make sure we capture it all of it. Is that okay with you?"*
>
> *"Again, the purpose of our interview is to get your feedback so that we can find ways to improve your experiences. We're looking for your honest feedback, so please give us the good, the bad, and the ugly! Do you have any questions for us before we begin?"*

### 3. Contextual interview body — the snorkel-to-scuba pattern

The body of the interview is structured as a **progressive deepening**:

> *"**Get a lay of the land**, start with key workflow/journey · Begin snorkeling · Start with the human element."*
>
> *"Then begin with the first key workflow. Go step-by-step through the journey. Capture JTBD, pains, aspirations along the way (NOT at the end!)."*
>
> *"**Scuba dive when you see potential buried treasure** · Go to root cause when you find a meaty JTBD/pain · IFD"*
>
> *"Move on to other key workflows as you exhaust the first · Go back to S/E jobs · Go back to 'squirrels' or skipped threads"*

The **snorkel-to-scuba metaphor** is the central methodological lesson: stay shallow across all workflows first, then dive deep where the customer shows emotional intensity (the "buried treasure" — pain that matters enough to monetize).

**"Capture along the way, NOT at the end"** is the second methodological lesson: don't save JTBD / pain / aspiration capture for a final summary. Tag them in real-time as the customer speaks.

### 4. Strong conclusion + wrap-up (15 minutes)

> *"At the end of the interview, take the time to skim back over your notes, summarize what you discussed and learned, and give the customer a final chance to correct and elaborate on your understanding."*

Three checkpoint actions in the wrap:

1. **Thank profusely** (*"be effusive here!"*) and discuss compensation if relevant.
2. **Ask for follow-up permission** — for both follow-on questions and **prototype review**: *"we are also prototyping solutions in a few weeks and would love to show you our ideas based on the insights you've provided here — would you mind if we reached back out down the road?"*
3. **Ask for referrals** — *"can you think of anyone else you think we should talk to?"*

### 5. Example discussion guide — key learning objectives

The guide provides a **worked example** for a hypothetical carrier (telco) study. The example is the cleanest template Subhasis et al. would adapt for Tek; the topic categories generalize directly:

**Example themes:**
- Overall sentiment
- Learn key customer journeys intimately (Journeys a-d)
- Gain consistent feedback on key topics: Relationship/Access to USC (In-store vs. digital), Bill Pay, DP+/Insurance, Referrals, Customer Appreciation Tactics, Birthday
- Conviction on the following: Top Jobs, Pains, Aspirations; Type of relationship desired; Benchmarking

**Top journeys to understand (in order of priority):**
1. Steady State: We do nothing for today
2. First 30-days and Onboarding
3. Danger Zone (even a year with us is enough time)
4. Triggering Event, Change to a Plan: Adding Line, Upgrading, Device, etc.
5. Offer Journeylines
6. Atypical Comms

### 6. Question bank — by category

The appendix is the **reusable question bank**. The categories the question bank covers:

- **Background** (about the customer's company, tenure, what drew them, what's changed)
- **Responsibilities & workflow** (with the directive *"ask for examples"*)
- **Social Jobs Question Set** (relationships at work, who they fear displeasing, last time customer was mad / delighted)
- **Emotional Jobs Question Set** (frustrations, pet peeves, one thing to change, why they've stuck)
- **Relationships** (who they work with, who their customers are, who they're motivated for)
- **Trends** (future generations doing this job, emerging payment methods impact)
- **Quantification of Pain Point Discovery — IFD** (Intensity Question Set, Frequency Question Set, Density Question Set)
- **Persistent Questions** (Why? How do you feel? What would make this easier?)
- **Initial Discovery Question Set** (*"Can you show me how you use / do <X>?"* — show-and-tell, not tell)
- **Customer Journeyline Question Set** (How did you first hear about X? Decision-making? What happens after use?)
- **Identifying Problems Question Set** (the *"Tell me a story about the last time…"* prompts that produce the deepest VOC data)

The deepest question framework is the **5-question chain** in Identifying Problems:

1. *"Tell me a story about the last time <significant problem context>?"* (story → unhypothesizeable detail)
2. *"What was hardest about that experience?"*
3. *"Why was that hard?"* (the **why** is what becomes marketing copy)
4. *"How do you solve it now?"* (if they aren't already trying — the problem isn't big enough)
5. *"Why is that not awesome?"* (the **answer is the Unique Value Proposition**)

## Friction notes

- **The guide does not name Tek, TekExpress, TekFlow, or any specific product.** It is generic Fortive Growth Accelerator (GA) methodology. The Tek-specific adaptation lives in [`Tx-Rx Solutions Platform VOC guide Draft _08_TEK-CONFIDENTIAL.pdf`](../uploads/artifacts/Tx-Rx Solutions Platform VOC guide Draft _08_TEK-CONFIDENTIAL.pdf) — pending `/document-pdf` processing.
- **The carrier / telco example** (USC, Bill Pay, DP+/Insurance, Birthday Communications) is wildly off-domain for Tek. Useful as a methodology illustration; useless as a topic template for HSS compliance customer interviews. Anyone using this guide for Tek work needs to substitute their own JTBD topics.
- **The "Past & Present, NOT the future"** ground rule is **methodologically inconsistent** with some of the question-bank prompts that ask for **trends** ("For future generations doing your job, what do they need to do…?") and **aspirations** ("Quantifying the Aspiration"). The guide reconciles this tension by treating aspirations as **present-tense desires** (not predictions) — but interviewers need to hold this carefully.
- **No JTBD persona templates are embedded in the document text.** The guide references "your customer persona template" as if it's an external attached artifact; the template itself is not in this `.docx`.
- **Audio + video recording consent** is bundled into the introduction script. For B2B engineering customer interviews (the Tek / NVIDIA / Intel / Infineon context in the existing decks), this consent flow may need adaptation — many engineering customers will refuse video recording in the lab environment.

## Annotations

> *"Customers don't buy the what, they buy the why. We'll use this answer to craft our marketing copy."* — GA guide, on the "Why?" follow-up in the Identifying Problems chain.

This is the **single most-actionable methodology principle in the guide**. It establishes that the *"why"* answer from the customer **directly becomes the marketing copy** for any product Tek builds in response.

> *"For us to take the risk of investing time and resources in an idea, we must be solving a problem so pressing customers are actively searching for solutions."* — GA guide, on the "How do you solve it now?" follow-up.

This is the **investment-decision threshold**. If the customer isn't already cobbling together a workaround, the pain isn't worth Tek's investment. This rule, applied to the existing empathy maps:
- **[Ampere "Has issues with Bitifeye"](../decks/designcon-2023-voc-summary/empathy-map-ampere.md)** — passes the threshold (active workaround = Bitifeye purchase).
- **[Intel "want to break monopolistic barrier"](../decks/designcon-2023-voc-summary/empathy-map-intel.md)** — passes (active workaround = M8040 control attempts).
- **[NVIDIA "moved to KS for Gen 5"](../decks/india-customer-voc-2023-march/empathy-map-nvidia.md)** — passes definitively (active workaround = competitor purchase).
- **[Infineon "Manual meas only" in Application Validation](../decks/india-customer-voc-2023-march/customer-workflow-infineon.md)** — passes (active workaround = manual labor).
- **[ElectroRent "Not aware of market movement"](../decks/designcon-2023-voc-summary/empathy-map-electrorent.md)** — does **not** pass the threshold by this rule (no active workaround visible). Reinforces the read that ElectroRent is a relationship-cultivation account, not a product-development driver.

> *"This is our Unique Value Proposition. We'll build something that solves our customer's problem, in a way that's better than their alternatives."* — GA guide, on the "Why is that not awesome?" follow-up.

The **UVP construction rule**. Applied to TekExpress redesign: the UVP candidates surfaced in the corpus so far (wizard save-replay loop, multi-instrument cross-vendor control, multi-lane parallel execution, application-validation automation) each need a *"why is the current workaround not awesome?"* answer to be marketable.
